# Sky Joud simulator: research brief (01)

Date: 2026-10-11. Scope: the operating environment of an F&B importer, manufacturer and distributor in Damascus, as inputs to `src/sim/scenario.ts` (MacroParams) and to the business engine. Built from four independent search sweeps (lenses: macro, sector, sd, tools). No primary PDF was read in full by any sweep (the sandbox proxy blocked direct fetches); every figure comes from search-engine extracts of the cited pages and must be re-verified against the source before it is hard-coded. All SYP figures state old pounds or new pounds (100 old = 1 new since 1 Jan 2026). Every number labelled "estimate" is the finder's or this brief's judgement, not a sourced datum. Six high-impact claims (Hormuz, Decree 110 duties, the 143 parallel rate, Decree 67 wages, grid hours, the Nasib truck ban) were re-checked with live web search on 11 Oct 2026; the verdicts are recorded in section 7 and the row corrections in section 6 items 16-21.

Corroboration key: "corroborated (2 lenses)" = two sweeps report the same variable within 10 percent; "conflict" = sweeps or sources disagree by more than 10 percent (both figures shown); "single lens" = one sweep only. The finder's own confidence (high/medium/low) is given first.

## 1. Summary

Damascus in October 2026 is a post-sanctions, high-inflation, energy-short market with a two-rate currency and thin banking. The legal overhang is gone (US comprehensive sanctions ended 1 Jul 2025, Caesar repealed 18 Dec 2025, SST rescinded 24 Aug 2026, EU lifted May 2025), so a European or Turkish supplier can sell to Sky Joud; the binding constraints are correspondent-bank access (payment share through formal channels is still small, prepayment of 50-100 percent is the norm), a parallel SYP rate about 11 percent weaker than the official 122 new SYP/USD in September (about 15 percent by 10 October, 140.5 parallel), and grid electricity that official and press sources put at 12-20 hours a day in 2026 (the 2-6 hours a day of the first draft was supported by no source on the 11 Oct 2026 check), with outages and the 30 Sep 2026 pipeline blast pushing factory energy cost to 60-95 new SYP/kWh on diesel versus 17-18 on the grid. Costs are being re-set in steps rather than drifting: diesel +40 percent on 13 Sep 2026, electricity tariff reset Nov 2025, public wages +50 percent May 2026, a new tariff schedule 1 Jun 2026 (Decree 110/2026) that cuts duties on most raw inputs, several of them fixed USD per ton, and raises them on processed foods with local substitutes, reportedly to 40-45 percent of CIF for biscuits, confectionery, chips and cold cuts (one unofficial site; a make-versus-import signal). Demand is growing with returnees (about 1.8 million refugee returns plus 2 million IDP returns since Dec 2024), tourism and a wage pulse, but most households sit below the WFP Minimum Expenditure Basket of about 26,500 new SYP (227 USD) a month, so packaged F&B volume is price-elastic and lumpy. The dominant external uncertainty for 2027 is the Strait of Hormuz: a protracted closure keeps Brent above 100 USD, Syrian fuel prices rising and freight surcharges high, although Syria's Mediterranean ports are not Hormuz-dependent and are gaining throughput. The simulator should therefore model administered-price step changes, a jump-diffusion FX path on the parallel rate, grid hours with sabotage shocks, route-closure events, and a customs regime toggle, and it must treat trade credit and DSO as business-supplied data because no public source documents them.

## 4. System dynamics: loops, equations, parameter ranges

Every loop below should exist explicitly in the business engine with a visible delay parameter. Citations are to the sd lens sources; textbook equations were confirmed from citing papers and course material, not from the books themselves.

**L1. Stock management with supply line (balancing; Sterman 2000 ch. 17, via Yasarcan papers and MIT 15.872).**
Chain: sales -> inventory gap -> orders -> supply line (lead time) -> deliveries -> inventory -> sales.
Equation: Orders = max(0, ExpectedLoss + (DesiredStock - Stock)/SAT + (DesiredSupplyLine - SupplyLine)/SLAT); DesiredSupplyLine = ExpectedLoss x ExpectedAcquisitionLag; DesiredStock = ExpectedDemand x DesiredCoverage; weight on supply line wsl = SAT/SLAT. wsl = 1 collapses stock and pipeline into one stock that cannot oscillate; wsl -> 0 reproduces textbook overshoot.
Sources: https://ie.bogazici.edu.tr/sites/ie.boun.edu.tr/files/publication/HakanYasarcan/SIMID/conference%20papers/5.A%20Parametric%20Analysis%20of%20the%20Effect%20of%20a%20Material%20Supply%20Line%20Delay%20in%20Stock%20Management.pdf ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-872-system-dynamics-ii-fall-2013/1b79076bb5c16be81bb3d3a4374e2a47_MIT15_872F13_ass2.pdf (high).
Parameters (monthly): SAT 1 month (0.5-2); wsl 0.2-0.5 for "as-is" managers, 1.0 for the disciplined policy; DesiredCoverage 1-2 months for imports, 0.5-1 for local goods; ExpectedAcquisitionLag = import lead time 1-3 months (sd finder estimates, converted from weekly textbook values).

**L2. Bullwhip (reinforcing amplification upstream; Lee, Padmanabhan and Whang 1997; Chen et al. 2000; Croson and Donohue 2006; Sterman 1989).**
Chain: retail demand step -> retailer forecast update -> retailer orders overshoot -> Sky Joud sees amplified orders -> import orders overshoot more -> glut -> order cut -> stockout later.
Equation (analytic bound for order-up-to with lead time L and p-period moving average): Var(Orders)/Var(Demand) >= 1 + 2L/p + 2L^2/p^2 (L = 2 months, p = 3 gives >= 3.2; L = 1, p = 6 gives >= 1.39). Behavioural ordering: anchor-and-adjust with demand smoothing theta, stock adjustment alpha_S, supply-line weight beta; Beer Game subjects underweight the supply line (beta well below 1, costs ~10x optimal); the finder's recalled estimates (theta ~0.36, alpha_S ~0.26, beta ~0.34) are unverified.
Sources: https://courses.ie.bilkent.edu.tr/ie102/wp-content/uploads/sites/12/2019/02/Lee-Padmanabhan-Whang-1997-MS.pdf ; https://scholars.cityu.edu.hk/en/publications/quantifying-the-bullwhip-effect-in-a-simple-supply-chain-the-impa/ ; https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/%5B34%5D.pdf ; https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/2184/SWP-1933-18213539.pdf?sequence=1 (high on structure, medium on numbers).
Syria mapping (switchable mechanisms): forecast updating = smoothing time; shortage gaming = retailers over-order when a border closes or FX jumps; batching = container MOQ quantisation; price variation = forward buying ahead of expected depreciation or tariff change. Sanity band: a wholesaler's variance ratio should sit above 1 but well below Beer Game extremes (Bray and Mendelson 2012: two-thirds of US firms show bullwhip, mean falling by a third since 1995; Cachon et al. 2007: wholesale shows it, retail mostly not). Reference mode: a +10 pct retail step should produce +40-50 pct factory/import order peak and an undershoot below baseline within ~6 months under naive ordering (Forrester 1958, secondary account: https://sloanreview.mit.edu/?p=5073 , medium).

**L3. FX pass-through to demand (balancing on volume, reinforcing on unit cost).**
Chain: parallel FX depreciation -> landed cost up (pass-through 0.6-0.8 within 1-2 months, estimate) -> retail price up -> real purchasing power down -> volume down (elasticity -1.2 to -2.0 discretionary, -0.3 to -0.6 staples, estimate) -> fixed cost per unit up -> margin down or further price up.
Equations: costIndex growth = costInflationMonthly + costFxPassThrough x max(0, fxLogChange) (already in scenario.ts); Volume = BaseVolume x (Price/PriceRef)^elasticity x ppIndex^incomeElasticity, with a perception delay (first-order, 1-2 months, estimate) on price.
Evidence: Lebanon 2019-21 (FAO): headline inflation 314 pct, food 441 pct, ~85 pct of consumption imported; Syria 2022 food inflation 71 pct (World Bank). Sources: https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/aa4fb60a-fd8d-4259-a19b-4c2d9788b97a/content ; https://www.orfonline.org/research/rethinking-resilient-food-systems-mitigating-food-supply-chain-shocks-in-the-mena-region (low, tails only).

**L4. Credit and cash squeeze ("Fixes that Fail", Senge; cash conversion cycle).**
Chain: extend retailer credit -> sales up -> receivables up -> cash down -> import orders cut (prepayment required) -> stockouts 2-3 months later -> sales down and customers churn -> cash down further.
Equations: CCC_imports = LeadTime + DIO + DSO - DPO, with DPO ~ 0 for imports under current banking (cash out at order); Receivables = INTEG(CreditSales - Collections), Collections as first-order delay with time DSO; bad-debt fraction as a parameter.
Sources: https://thesystemsthinker.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Systems-Archetypes-II-TRSA02E.pdf ; https://www.gurufocus.com/term/cash-conversion-cycle/SYY ; https://bsouria.com/en/articles/importing-exporting-syria-guide-2026 (medium/low).
Parameters: DSO 25-45 days; DIO 20-45 days fast movers, 60-130 days imported dry goods; DPO 0 imports, 35-70 days domestic; LC/open-account return scenario raises import DPO to 30-60 days over 2026-28 (all estimates; US benchmarks are the efficient end).

**L5. Growth and underinvestment (Senge archetype).**
Chain: sales growth -> warehouse/fleet/line capacity saturates -> service level falls -> lost customers -> growth slows; perceived performance -> perceived need to invest (delayed, goal erodes) -> investment -> capacity.
Equations: CapacityGap = DesiredCapacity - Capacity; Investment = CapacityGap/CapacityAdjustmentTime when PerceivedPerformance < Standard; Standard = SMOOTH(Performance, GoalErosionTime) if the eroding-goal switch is on.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_and_underinvestment ; https://thesystemsthinker.com/growth-and-underinvestment-is-your-company-playing-with-a-wooden-racket/ (high on structure).
Parameters: capacity acquisition lag 3-12 months; goal erosion time 6-24 months (estimates).

**L6. Energy and capacity (balancing on output, reinforcing on cost).**
Chain: gridHours down -> self-generation share up (1 - gridHours/operatingHours) -> energy cost per kWh up (diesel/3.2 + 10-15 new SYP O&M) -> COGS up -> price up (L3) or margin down; outage hours -> chilled spoilage up (2-5 pct/month) -> write-offs.
Parameters: grid 17-18 new SYP/kWh; generator 60-95 new SYP/kWh at diesel 150-175 new SYP/L; energy 30-50 pct of COGS for energy-intensive lines (estimates; see Macro and Sector tables).

**L7. Inventory-workforce oscillation in manufacturing (Sterman 2000 ch. 19; MIT 15.872).**
Chain: inventory gap -> desired production -> desired workforce -> hiring/firing (delay) -> production -> inventory. Longer hiring delay makes the oscillation more pronounced.
Parameters converted to months: manufacturing cycle time ~1; WIP adjustment ~0.5; inventory adjustment 2 (1-3); desired coverage ~1 (0.5 processing + 0.5 safety); demand smoothing ~2; labour adjustment ~3. Sources: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-872-system-dynamics-ii-fall-2013/1b79076bb5c16be81bb3d3a4374e2a47_MIT15_872F13_ass2.pdf ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.11434 (medium; course values, not textbook table).

**L8. Expectation formation.** ExpectedDemand = INTEG((Demand - ExpectedDemand)/TimeToFormExpectations, Demand); optional TREND = (input - avval)/(averageTime x |avval|) for nominal growth under inflation (Vensim docs; Sterman 1987). Time to form expectations 1-4 months as a Monte Carlo parameter. Shorter smoothing raises bullwhip; longer raises stockout risk after a step. Source: https://vensim.com/documentation/20480.html (high).

**Delays.** Sea freight plus customs: third-order or pipeline delay with mean = lead time and order n = mean^2/variance (mean 60 days, sd 20 -> n = 9, nearly a pipeline); receivables and perception: first-order. Source: https://build.openmodelica.org/Documentation/BusinessSimulation.Stocks.DelayN.html (high).

**Time step.** Vensim: dt below 1/3 of the shortest time constant or Euler fabricates oscillation. A monthly step with SAT = 1 month violates this: sub-step at dt = 0.25 month or use exact discrete updates (alpha = 1 - exp(-dt/SAT); Erlang convolution for the third-order delay). Source: https://vensim.com/documentation/ref_time_step.html (high).

**Validation protocol (Barlas 1996; Oliva 2003; Theil statistics).** Order: structure tests -> structure-oriented behaviour tests (extreme conditions: zero demand, infinite lead time, FX x10, no negative stocks; behaviour sensitivity) -> behaviour-pattern tests. Calibrate sub-models separately (ordering, cash, capacity) against Sky Joud's monthly history; report RMSE and Theil Um/Uv/Uc, flag runs with bias share Um above ~0.2 (rule of thumb, not verified from the text). Prefer reproducing behaviour modes (oscillation period, amplification) over point fit. Test fixture: the Beer Game (holding 0.50, backlog 1.00 USD per case-week, 12 cases initial, demand 4 -> 8 at week 5; delay structure disagrees across sources: 2+2 weeks vs 1+3) should reproduce upstream amplification before Syrian parameters are loaded. Sources: https://avesis.acibadem.edu.tr/yayin/1cc89ed0-1ffc-4239-be4b-c399e09cc297/formal-aspects-of-model-validity-and-validation-in-system-dynamics ; https://people.tamu.edu/~roliva/research/sd/calibration.html ; https://vensim.com/documentation/extended_payoff_reporting.html (high).

**Fisher product typology (HBR 1997, secondary).** Functional staples: forecast error ~10 pct, stockout tolerance 1-2 pct, margin 5-20 pct; innovative launches: error 40-100 pct, markdown 10-25 pct, margin 20-60 pct. Carry two SKU archetypes. Source: https://www.scl.gatech.edu/sites/default/files/downloads/gtscl_ddscs_20150714.pdf (high).

## 5. Tools: borrow, avoid, libraries

Borrow:
- Monte Carlo defaults from Vensim: N = 200 runs, fixed seed (1234), Latin Hypercube sampling, percentile bands computed per time step by sorting runs, nested 50/75/95 bands (https://vensim.com/documentation/sensitivity.html ; https://www.vensim.com/documentation/ref_sensi_graph.html , high). Insight Maker's densest 50/75/95/100 pct bands with Excel export are the same convention for non-technical users (https://insightmaker.com/sensitivitytesting , high).
- Presentation: fan charts with a median line are the best-understood joint display of expectation and uncertainty and reduce de-anchoring after forecast errors (Bank of England WP 1196, 2026, https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/working-paper/2026/anchors-aweigh-the-effect-of-communicating-forecast-uncertainty , medium); for single-number outcomes use quantile dotplots of 20-50 dots or a CDF, which beat density plots and text (Kay et al. 2016; Fernandes et al. 2018, 97 pct of optimal payoff, https://idl.uw.edu/papers/uncertainty-bus , medium). Always state the range in words next to the chart because people underestimate uncertainty.
- Sensitivity: two tornado views, one-at-a-time P10 to P90 swings around the P50 base per decision option shown side by side (Tufte notes), and a Monte Carlo tornado sized by Spearman rank correlation; label percentiles "1-in-10 bad", "typical", "1-in-10 good"; warn that stacking all lows is not a realistic case (https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/tornado-plots/ , medium). Put SAT and wsl at the top of the sensitivity list (SD Society 2010 P1260).
- Scenario data model from SAP IBP: long-lived named versions (macro regimes), lightweight scenario overrides, throwaway what-if runs, "promote scenario to base" (https://learning.sap.com/courses/mastering-the-advanced-configuration-in-sap-ibp/creating-and-comparing-versions-and-scenarios-1 , medium). From Coupa/o9: show revenue, margin and working capital in every scenario (financial lens), ignore their ROI claims.
- Game mechanics from Capsim/Cesim/Harvard sims: practice rounds before real runs, pro-forma financials before committing, one headline score plus a few KPIs, supplier choice as cost x lead time x capacity; Beer Game anchor-and-adjust rule for the ordering loop (https://forio.com/catalog/harvard-root-beer-game-supply-chain-simulation , medium).
- Decision output as "what would have to be true" (Lafley, Martin, Rivkin, Siggelkow 2012): report the parameter thresholds at which an option beats baseline in more than X pct of runs (https://cd-www.rotman.utoronto.ca/Connect/MediaCentre/NewsReleases/Bringing-Science-to-Strategy , high).
- Sourcing-mix lever and cost-per-unit-delivered KPI, since WFP's Optimus MILP got 74 pct of cost for 96 pct of nutrition in Syria 2016 and 12-17 pct savings in Iraq (https://innovation.wfp.org/project/optimus , medium): proven lever size in this market.
- Design stance: pre-built, domain-specific model with few owner-editable parameters and macro sliders, framed as a mental-model test with ranges, not predictions (Arthur and Winch, https://archives.albany.edu/downloads/2b88qw96h?locale=de , medium); make the owner a co-builder by exposing the causal loop diagram and logging assumptions, because model use without involvement did not change managers' attention or confidence (McCardle-Keurentjes et al. 2018, https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/sysdyn/v34y2018i1-2p354-384.html , medium).
- Disruption taxonomy (demand, supply, transport, node) and perishability ageing chains from humanitarian food SD papers (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10082632/ , medium); no SD or digital-twin case exists for food distribution under currency collapse, so the Sky Joud structure is novel.

Avoid:
- `simulation` npm 9.0.0 (Insight Maker engine): AGPL, would bind the app (https://www.npmjs.com/package/simulation , high).
- `lhs` npm 0.0.0 (2013, abandoned): write Latin Hypercube in-house (~20 lines). `sd.js` (archived Sep 2021). Adaptive ODE libraries (odex, ode45-cash-karp): unnecessary at a monthly step; a fixed-step Euler or RK4 with sub-stepping is standard.
- Simul8-style 5 replications with 95 pct CI: too few for scenario distributions; use 200+.
- Commercial SD/DES/planning suites as the engine: AnyLogic Professional 12,390-18,990 USD per seat (unverified Scribd list; Monte Carlo likely Professional-only), Stella Architect 3,999 USD perpetual or 1,929 USD/yr, Simul8 4,995-9,569 USD/user/yr (quote-based), SAP IBP, o9 and Coupa quote-based and deterministic. Vensim Professional 1,195 USD and DSS 1,995 USD are affordable but require SD skill and sensitivity is not in PLE (tier not directly verified).
- Claims that game performance transfers to real decisions: evidence is classroom-based and ambiguous; value comes from structured debriefs and explicit assumption comparison.

Viable JS/TS libraries (versions read from registry.npmjs.org on 2026-10-11, tools lens, high):
- Random: `pure-rand` 8.4.2 (MIT, seeded TS PRNG, Jul 2026) preferred; or `d3-random` 3.0.1 (ISC, seedable via randomLcg) with `seedrandom` 3.0.5 (MIT, 2019); `@stdlib/random-base-normal` 0.2.2 (Apache-2.0, Feb 2026). The existing `src/sim/rng.ts` already covers seeding and normals; keep it unless a distribution is missing.
- Statistics: `simple-statistics` 7.12.1 (ISC, Sep 2026) for quantiles and Spearman; `mathjs` 15.2.0 (Apache-2.0) if matrix work appears; `jStat` 1.9.6 (MIT, 2022, stale) only if needed.
- SD engines: hand-written stock-flow core in TypeScript (recommended for a ~30-equation monthly model); Simlin (Apache-2.0, Rust to WASM, but `@simlin/engine` is not on npm, build from source); SDEverywhere (`@sdeverywhere/cli` 0.7.49, MIT, Sep 2026) if models are authored in Vensim/XMILE and compiled to JS/WASM, with its regression harness; PySD 462 stars (MIT, Python) for offline cross-checks.
- Parallelism: run Monte Carlo batches in Web Workers (AnyLogic parallelises per core; the browser equivalent).
